# BANTAY BUWIS PH — Complete Launch Content Pack

A civic commentary Facebook page for ordinary Filipino taxpayers.

**Editorial ground rules (what keeps this page safe and credible):**
- Every post is framed as **opinion, commentary, or a question** — never as a news report.
- **No invented statistics, no fabricated documents, no allegations against named individuals.**
- Posts speak to widely shared, verifiable general realities (VAT exists on purchases,
  flooding recurs, classrooms are crowded, commutes are long).
- The page is transparently labeled as an opinion page, not a news organization.
- When you later want to cite specific findings (audits, budgets, cases), link the actual
  primary source document and quote it exactly — never paraphrase numbers from memory.

## 7. POSTING & GROWTH STRATEGY (the honest playbook)

### Timing (Philippine audience)
- **6:00–8:00 AM** — commute scroll (best for Posts 1, 3)
- **11:30 AM–1:00 PM** — lunch break
- **8:00–10:00 PM** — prime engagement window (best for Posts 2, 4, 5)
- One post per day maximum. Day 1: Post 1 (defines the page), then Posts 3, 5, 4, 2.

### Engagement that builds real community
- Reply to comments within the first hour — this is the single biggest reach lever.
- The crowdsourcing hooks (commute times, flood map, teacher stories) create
  **follow-up content from real people** — the most credible content you can post.
- Heart/care-react thoughtful comments; never reward toxic ones with engagement.

### Sharing to groups — the safe way
Share only to groups where **you are a genuine, active member** and where the topic
fits the group's purpose and rules (commuter groups, community/barangay groups,
taxpayer/consumer groups). Never spam-blast, never use fake accounts, never
misrepresent who runs the page. Authentic sharing grows slower but survives.

### How to level this up with real receipts later
When ready to cite specifics: download the actual document (e.g., an agency's
published annual audit report from coa.gov.ph, the GAA from dbm.gov.ph, a
published court decision), quote the exact line, screenshot the page, and link
the source in the caption. Fact-anchored posts should say exactly what the
document says — nothing more.
