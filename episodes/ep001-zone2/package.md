# EP001 — "Why You Gas Out After 2KM" (Zone 2 Science)

## 🔬 1. Science / Experiment Anchor
- **Fact:** Elite endurance athletes (Seiler's polarized-training research) run ~80% of weekly mileage at easy conversational "Zone 2" pace — this builds mitochondrial density and fat oxidation, the engine behind their speed. Beginners live in Zone 3–4 ("gray zone"): too hard to build the aerobic engine, too easy to build speed → constant gasping, no progress.
- **Pinoy Hook:** PH heat/humidity adds 10–15 bpm at the same pace — copying paces from cool-country influencers puts Filipino runners in the gray zone every run. Zone 2 is the survival strategy for 38°C heat index.

> **[0–3s | direct, no fluff]** Gassing out after just 2 kilometers? [beat] Listen. You're not weak — your pace is wrong.
> **[3–8s | firm, calling it out]** Look at this. 178 heart rate on your so-called "easy run." That's not easy. That's the gray zone — too fast to build endurance, too slow to build speed.
> **[8–16s | slower, weighty]** Here's what no one tells you: elite runners do almost **80 percent** of their running... [pause] ...slow. So slow you could hold a full conversation while running. That's Zone 2.
> **[16–24s | science mode, certain]** In Zone 2, you grow your mitochondria — the engines inside your muscles. They learn to burn fat instead of pure sugar. Bigger fuel tank, longer runs, less gasping.
