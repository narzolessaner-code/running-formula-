## 🔬 1. Science / Experiment Anchor
- **Fact:** The landmark carbon-plate study (Hoogkamer et al., 2018, on the original Vaporfly) measured ~4% better running economy vs traditional racing shoes — but it was tested on fast runners. Follow-up research shows the benefit varies per runner ("responders" and "non-responders"), and evidence suggests the advantage shrinks at slower, recreational paces.
- **Pinoy Hook:** Carbon shoes in the PH cost ₱9,000–₱14,000. The question every runner in the running era asks: are they worth it if your pace is 6:30–7:30/km? Nobody tests this fairly here. We're the first — same legs, same road, same effort, and the watch is the judge.

## 🧪 THE PROTOCOL (do this before scripting the results)
- Same route (3km, flat, minimal traffic), same time of day, 1–2 days apart, same sleep and breakfast as much as possible.
- Run A: budget shoes (₱1,500 class). Run B: carbon plate.
- HOLD EFFORT CONSTANT, not pace: talk-test effort (5–6/10) the whole run.
- Record per run: average pace, average HR, and how the legs feel afterward (1–10).
- The comparison: at the SAME effort, which gives a faster pace / lower HR?
